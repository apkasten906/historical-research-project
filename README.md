# Historical Research Project

Project to establish a historical research program aimed at supporting social media content generation and interactive digital experiences.

## Purpose

This repository provides the research-management and digital-methods framework for historically grounded projects that may support multiple outputs, including:

- scholarly and Digital Humanities research;
- long-form and short-form historical media;
- interactive digital experiences and games;
- public-facing source, map, timeline, and data products.

The research layer should remain intellectually independent from any particular commercial output. A historical claim should not become more certain because a story, video, or game requires it.

## Research principles

1. **Evidence first.** Distinguish source evidence, scholarly interpretation, inference, tradition/legend, and invention.
2. **Traceability.** Important claims should be traceable to identifiable sources.
3. **Structured uncertainty.** Conflicting or incomplete evidence should be recorded rather than silently resolved.
4. **Reusable research.** Where appropriate, research should be structured so that data, chronologies, prosopography, networks, and geographic information can support more than one output.
5. **Research/output separation.** Scholarly research data and methods should be distinguishable from journalistic framing, narrative design, game mechanics, and fictional content.
6. **Rights awareness.** Source, archive, image, database, publication, and commercial-use rights should be recorded early.
7. **Open research where appropriate.** Potential scholarly datasets and publications should be identified separately from proprietary media/game IP.

## Research programmes

### BRG — Bruges 1488

The first research programme examines Bruges and the Burgundian-Habsburg Netherlands during the political crisis surrounding Maximilian I's captivity in 1488.

Current areas include:

- prosopography and political networks;
- civic government, guilds, offices, and institutions;
- Maximilian's household and Burgundian noble networks;
- religious institutions, relics, ritual, and political culture;
- chronology and event reconstruction;
- places, movement, and historical geography;
- source criticism and evidence classification;
- potential Digital Humanities methods and research datasets.

See [`research-programs/bruges-1488/`](research-programs/bruges-1488/).

## Issue identifiers

GitHub issue numbers are repository-wide and should not be used as conceptual research identifiers.

Use prefixes in issue titles:

- `HRP-###` — programme-wide research infrastructure, methodology, governance, or tooling;
- `BRG-###` — Bruges 1488 research-programme development;
- future programmes should receive their own three-letter prefix.

Existing subject-level research codes such as `RES-###` and `WKS-###` may continue to identify detailed historical research items in their original project context. They should not be renumbered merely to fit this repository.

## Current development sequence

The Bruges 1488 academic/Digital Humanities track is intentionally gated:

1. produce a concise research concept brief;
2. obtain informal Digital Humanities feedback;
3. audit the existing research assets;
4. define a bounded pilot research question;
5. define research-data, publication, and IP boundaries;
6. map academic partners and realistic grant programmes;
7. approach relevant specialist research groups only after the concept is academically sharpened;
8. make a grant go/no-go decision before proposal writing.

This sequence is designed to test whether the existing work constitutes a genuine research contribution before attempting to fit it to a funding call.
