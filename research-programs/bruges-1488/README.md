# BRG — Bruges 1488 Research Programme

## Scope

This programme investigates Bruges and the Burgundian-Habsburg Netherlands during the political crisis surrounding Maximilian I's captivity in 1488.

The programme is intended to test whether the existing body of project research can be developed into a genuine Digital Humanities / historical research contribution while continuing to support public-history media and interactive digital experiences.

## Immediate academic-development goals

- define a concise research concept suitable for expert feedback;
- inventory the existing research assets and distinguish evidence from interpretation and invention;
- identify one bounded pilot research question;
- determine appropriate Digital Humanities methods and data structures;
- define research-data, publication, licensing, and commercial-IP boundaries;
- identify realistic academic partners and grant programmes;
- decide whether a formal grant proposal is justified.

## Current candidate research themes

- political and institutional networks during the 1488 crisis;
- prosopography of Maximilian's household, Burgundian nobles, civic actors, clergy, and intermediaries;
- civic institutions, guild structures, offices, and negotiation mechanisms;
- religious institutions, relics, ritual, and political legitimacy;
- chronology, movement, captivity, negotiation, and violence;
- historical geography of Bruges and connected centres;
- source criticism and machine-readable evidence classification.

## Relationship to project outputs

The research programme is not identical to either the media series or the RPG. Research outputs may inform both, while narrative design, game mechanics, fictional characters, commercial branding, and other proprietary production assets remain separate layers.
